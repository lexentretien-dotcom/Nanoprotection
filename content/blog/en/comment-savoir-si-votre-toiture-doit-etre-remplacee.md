title: "How do you know if your roof needs replacing?"
description: "The signs that an asphalt shingle roof is reaching the end of its life, and the ones that still leave some room."

---

An asphalt shingle roof ages slowly, and unevenly. The south-facing slope does not look like the one that stays in the shade. Before deciding anything, it is better to observe what you actually see.

## Start by looking, not guessing

From the ground, with binoculars, you can already spot a lot without going on the roof. Never climb onto a slippery roof or without safety equipment.

## Signs that point toward replacement

Shingles cracked deeply, curling or lifting over large areas are a serious signal. The same goes for repeated leaks, moisture stains on the ceiling or decking that sags.

When the granule layer has almost disappeared in places and the asphalt is bare, the shingle no longer protects as intended.

## Signs that still leave options

Surface wear, a slight loss of granules or a few isolated damaged shingles do not automatically mean everything must be replaced. In these cases, a targeted repair, or preservation if the roof is sound overall, can be considered.

## Why an inspection changes everything

Only an on-site inspection can tell normal wear from a real problem. An honest recommendation separates three things: what must be repaired, what can be preserved and what must be replaced.
